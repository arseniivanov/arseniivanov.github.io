# Research Incentives

This week has been a bit of an eye-opener for a lot of researchers and people in science. Similar to artists, and all other informational roles whose outputs have been gradually replaced, it was shown that your math degree is much, much less worth from an output perspective. Terrence Tao and other fields medalists are coming out with [incentive misalignment statements](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/09/11/a-severe-misalignment-of-ai-in-mathematics/). Compute and capital will win over you.
Reading HN, discord and reddit forums in the affected communities had reactions felt like a run-through of the five stages of grief at silicon-valley-speed:

- It's just an obscure part of the proof, also its completely unreadable and so many lines of code its pointless and has little value.
* They learned through back-channels that the NYU and Anthropic groups were close to a solution, they stole their solution from the chats and there are no repercussions! OpenAI asked the NYU author to remove the Antrophic author from the paper if he wants the 1M$ prize? Total misconduct!
* Okay, if AI does the proofs and we still choose the questions and explain why the answers matter, then we're still the researchers, right? I'll give up being the one who solves it, as long as being the one who understands it.
* My role and identity as a researcher cannot be measured by the output of my work any longer, because since I don't own the compute, I don't own the capital, I will inevitably inherently be useless in producing value here. And with the current incentives, no-one cares if I understood something, so my role in my occupation is reduced to social courtesy and slop creation in order to get my paycheck.
* Tying my value and meaning to outcomes and ego in a world where informational roles will be fully automated by capital and compute is pointless and will only lead me to depression, I should identify with the process, or find meaningful goals outside of my occupation to define me. 

I think that this could benefit the field in the long-term. It will create a much better incentives for knowledge and academic work in general. Instead of spamming papers on incremental, hill-climbed additions with reward-hacked benchmarks and harnesses, researchers will have to focus on thinking outside of the existing model embedding space, and focus on the process of learning to both ask and answer better questions.

However there are some problems standing in the way of this liberation:
* A lot of people still depend on informational work for survival in a capitalist system.
* There is a human social desire for status. Stamping your name on papers, proofs, inventions or breakthroughs can define your opportunities for other human social aspects, like social networking, resource accumulation and sexual reproduction.
* The incentives of research will take years to catch up, and a lot of capital-based incentives are stacked against this change. There will still be conferences for multiple years where you will be incentivized to spam hill-climbed incremental results. Your supervisors will still face pressure from above to make you publish this slop in order to get more funding.

So what does this point to?

The likely outcome I am seeing is that we will automate research work faster than we fix the credit assignment system. Papers will get cheaper, prestige has to remain scarce to be meaningful, things will get more competitive, and institutions/people in power will find increasingly elaborate ways to explain why the people already holding the power still deserve it, why they should get the attention, etc.

We can see this starting to happen, whether intentional or not given the reasoning about limiting AI spam, with the paper limits from new contributors that the big conferences are putting up: 1 [coin flip](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1sj76a2/just_did_an_analysis_on_iclr_2025_vs_2026_scores/) for newcomers, 20 for successful people already in the system in [ICLR 2027](https://blog.iclr.cc/2026/09/02/submission-policies-for-iclr-2027/?utm_source=chatgpt.com).  [AISTATS 2027](https://virtual.aistats.org/Conferences/2027/CallForPapers#submission-quotas) similarly has 3 for newcomers, 12 for people with existing clout/status.
